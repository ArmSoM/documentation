# ArmSoM Camera Applications

ArmSoM Camera Applications support all ArmSoM boards based on **RK3588, RK3588S or RK3576**. After you enable the appropriate camera overlay, the applications list the detected IMX219 and OV13850 sensors and the resolutions and frame rates reported by their drivers.

:::info Included in SDK images

Debian 12 arm64 desktop images built with the default SDK configuration include `armsom-camera` and `armsom-camera-desktop`. Use Cheese, guvcview or the command line directly. Server images include `armsom-camera` for use over SSH or serial.

:::

Use **Cheese** for quick photos and videos with separate resolutions for each. Use **guvcview** when you want to set the frame rate as well as the resolution. Its controls and preview have separate windows.

This guide uses **CM7S with the Debian 12 XFCE desktop**. On Ubuntu or another desktop image, search the applications menu for **Cheese** or **guvcview**, open it, and follow the same steps. Menu locations and button labels can vary with the desktop environment or display language.

## 1. Connect a camera {#prepare}

1. Power off the board and connect the camera ribbon cable. Enable the device-tree overlay for the sensor and connector, then reboot. For wiring and configuration, see the **MIPI-CSI** section in your product’s user manual.
2. In XFCE, open **Applications → Multimedia** and select **Cheese** or **guvcview**. On GNOME, search for the application by name in **Activities**. Close one camera application before opening the other.
3. On boards with several cameras, look for the IMX219 or OV13850 sensor name. The connector or module number distinguishes two sensors of the same model. On AIM7 with AIM-IO, the connector names match CAM0 through CAM3 on the carrier.

<figure className="armsom-doc-figure">

![XFCE Applications menu showing Cheese and guvcview under Multimedia](/img/advanced_manual/armsom-camera/desktop-menu.webp)

<figcaption>① Applications　② Multimedia　③ Choose Cheese or guvcview</figcaption>
</figure>

:::info About the screenshots

The screenshots show the English button labels. Images with ArmSoM camera support show sensor names and the native modes reported by the driver.

:::

On CM7S, the two sensor drivers report these modes. The capture resolution is independent of the size of the preview window.

| Camera | Resolution (pixels) | Maximum frame rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IMX219 | 3280 × 2464 | 15 fps |
| IMX219 | 1640 × 1232, 1920 × 1080, 640 × 480 | 30 fps |
| OV13850 | 4224 × 3136 | 7.5 fps |
| OV13850 | 2112 × 1568 | 30 fps |

## 2. Cheese {#cheese}

### 2.1 Select a camera and resolution {#cheese-settings}

Open **Cheese** and wait for the preview. Click **① ☰** at the top right and choose **② Preferences**. The same menu includes **③ Fullscreen**.

<figure className="armsom-doc-figure">

![Cheese menu with Preferences and Fullscreen highlighted](/img/advanced_manual/armsom-camera/cheese-menu.webp)

<figcaption>Open the ☰ menu at the top right.</figcaption>
</figure>

In **Preferences → Webcam**, use the three fields shown here.

<figure className="armsom-doc-figure">

![Device, Photo resolution and Video resolution on the Webcam tab](/img/advanced_manual/armsom-camera/cheese-settings.webp)

<figcaption>① Camera　② Photo resolution　③ Video resolution</figcaption>
</figure>

Open **① Device**. This CM7S has two entries, **IMX219 (m01, 4-0010)** and **OV13850 (m00, 1-0010)**. Choose one to switch the live preview to that camera.

<figure className="armsom-doc-figure">

![Cheese device selector showing the two detected cameras on CM7S](/img/advanced_manual/armsom-camera/cheese-devices.webp)

<figcaption>① IMX219　② OV13850</figcaption>
</figure>

Set **② Photo resolution** and **③ Video resolution** separately. For example, choose **3280 × 2464** for full-resolution IMX219 photos and **1640 × 1232** for video with the full field of view at up to 30 fps. Click **Close** to return to the preview.

<figure className="armsom-doc-figure">

![The four photo resolutions available for IMX219 in Cheese](/img/advanced_manual/armsom-camera/cheese-modes.webp)

<figcaption>① Open the photo resolution list and choose the size to save.</figcaption>
</figure>

:::tip Choosing a frame rate

Cheese negotiates the frame rate for the selected video resolution. To select a specific frame rate in the interface, use [guvcview](#guvcview).

:::

### 2.2 Take a photo or record video {#cheese-capture}

After selecting the camera and photo resolution, close Preferences to return to the preview. Click **① Photo** at the bottom left, then click **② the shutter** to take a picture. If the countdown is enabled, Cheese takes the photo when it ends. Double-click the saved photo in **③ the thumbnail strip** to view it.

<figure className="armsom-doc-figure">

![Cheese Photo mode, shutter and thumbnail strip](/img/advanced_manual/armsom-camera/cheese-photo.webp)

<figcaption>① Photo mode　② Shutter　③ Captured photo</figcaption>
</figure>

Click **① Video** to switch modes, then click the record button in the middle to start recording. **③ The timer** shows the elapsed time. Click **② Stop (■)** to finish and save the video. When its thumbnail appears, double-click it to play the recording.

<figure className="armsom-doc-figure">

![Cheese in Video mode with the stop button and recording timer highlighted](/img/advanced_manual/armsom-camera/cheese-video.webp)

<figcaption>① Video mode　② Stop and save　③ Elapsed time</figcaption>
</figure>

:::tip Where Cheese saves photos and videos

On the CM7S XFCE image shown here, Cheese saves both photos and videos in `~/.gnome2/cheese/media/`. Open the file manager, press `Ctrl+L`, paste the path, and press Enter. `~` is the home directory of the signed-in desktop user.

If the desktop has configured Pictures and Videos folders, Cheese saves captures in the `Webcam` subfolder of each by default. On an English desktop these paths are usually `~/Pictures/Webcam/` and `~/Videos/Webcam/`.

:::

### 2.3 Fullscreen preview {#cheese-fullscreen}

Open **☰ → Fullscreen** at the top right, or press **F11** in Cheese. Press **F11** again to return to the normal window. The capture resolution stays the same when you resize the preview.

## 3. guvcview {#guvcview}

### 3.1 Select a camera {#guvc-settings}

</figure>

Choose the sensor you want to use. Switching cameras can open a **start new** dialog. Click **restart** to reopen the current guvcview session with the selected camera.

First, open **Resolution**. For this IMX219 example, select **1640x1232**. Then open **Frame Rate** above it and select **30/1 fps** (30 frames per second). If you select **3280x2464**, its corresponding rate is **15/1 fps**. You can leave **Camera Output** at **NV12**.

### 3.3 Take a photo or record video {#guvc-capture}

After choosing the camera, resolution and frame rate, wait for a steady preview. Click **① Cap. Image (I)** at the top of the **control window** to capture and save one photo. For video, click **② Cap. Video (V)** to start recording, then click **Stop Video (V)** in the same place to finish and save the file. The example uses OV13850 at **2112 × 1568 and 30 fps**.

<figcaption>① Stop recording　② Check the output path</figcaption>

:::tip Where guvcview saves photos and videos

In this example, guvcview saves captures in the desktop user’s home directory, `/home/armsom/`. Open **Home** in the file manager to find the photo `my_photo-1.jpg` and the video `my_video-1.mkv`. If you have changed the filename or destination, the status bar at the bottom of the control window shows the full path in use.

:::

On XFCE, right-click the title bar of the window **showing the camera feed** and choose **Fullscreen**. The preview fills the display while keeping its aspect ratio. To return to windowed mode, click the preview to give it focus, then press **Alt + F11**.

## 4. Command line on desktop and Server images {#server-cli}

Open a terminal on the desktop, or log in over SSH or serial on a Server image. Use the commands below to select a camera, resolution and frame rate. Photos and recordings can be taken without a monitor.

### 4.1 List cameras and modes {#server-list}

```bash
armsom-camera list
```

Choose a camera ID and one of the resolution and frame-rate combinations listed for it. The CM7S examples below use `imx219-m01` and `ov13850-m00`. If an AIM7 lists two sensors of the same model as `cam0` and `cam1`, pass the desired connector ID to `--camera`.

### 4.2 Take a JPEG photo {#server-photo}

Use the full-resolution IMX219 mode to save one photo as `imx219.jpg` in your home directory.

```bash
armsom-camera photo --camera imx219-m01 --size 3280x2464 --fps 15 -o ~/imx219.jpg
```

To switch to OV13850, select its ID and a mode listed for that sensor.

```bash
armsom-camera photo --camera ov13850-m00 --size 2112x1568 --fps 30 -o ~/ov13850.jpg
```

### 4.3 Record an MP4 video {#server-video}

Record 10 seconds with OV13850 at 2112 × 1568 and 30 fps. When the command finishes, the recording is available at `~/ov13850.mp4`. The duration is in seconds. Omit `--duration` to record until you press `Ctrl+C` to stop and save.

```bash
armsom-camera video --camera ov13850-m00 --size 2112x1568 --fps 30 --duration 10 -o ~/ov13850.mp4
```

### 4.4 Preview on a monitor connected to the board {#server-preview}

Connect a monitor to an active display output on the board, then run the command below. The `--display kms` option uses the board’s DRM/KMS display output without starting a desktop. If you run the command over SSH or serial, the preview appears on the monitor attached to the board. Press `Ctrl+C` to close it.

```bash
armsom-camera preview --camera imx219-m01 --size 1640x1232 --fps 30 --display kms
```

To open a preview window from a desktop terminal, run the same command without `--display kms`.
